\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiments}

In this section, we present comprehensive empirical evaluations of Fed-LUNAR across four canonical IoT intrusion detection benchmarks executed on dedicated edge server hardware. We evaluate detection accuracy, Non-IID Dirichlet robustness, component ablation dynamics, and edge systems feasibility.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{subsec:exp_setup}

\subsubsection{Testbed Hardware Environment}
All federated training and evaluation experiments are conducted on an enterprise research edge server running Linux Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, equipped with an Intel Core i9-13900K processor (32 vCPUs @ 5.8\,GHz), 62\,GB system DDR5 RAM, and a physical NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5090 GPU (32\,GB GDDR7 VRAM, CUDA 13.0, PyTorch 2.11).

\subsubsection{Canonical IoT Datasets}
To ensure rigorous evaluation across diverse IoT environments and attack topologies, we evaluate four benchmark datasets pre-scaled under standardized one-class protocols:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{BoTIoT}}~\cite{koroniotis2019towards}: Captured in a smart home IoT testbed, containing massive volumetric DDoS, DoS, OS reconnaissance, and keylogging traffic ($D = 26$ flow features).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{EdgeIIoTset}}~\cite{ferrag2022edgeiiotset}: Multi-protocol industrial IoT (IIoT) telemetry comprising Modbus, MQTT, and CoAP telemetry subjected to ransomware, SQL injection, and DDoS ($D = 52$ features).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{CICIoT2023}}~\cite{neto2023ciciot2023}: Large-scale IoT attack dataset encompassing 33 distinct attack profiles with high-rate volumetric floods and brute-force scans ($D = 44$ features).
    \item \textbf{\texttt{N\_BaIoT}}~\cite{meidan2018n}: Real-world commercial hardware botnet dataset captured from 9 infected IoT devices (e.g., Doorbell, Security Camera) exhibiting Mirai and Bashlite infection stages ($D = 115$ high-dimensional statistical stream features).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Non-IID Partitioning Protocol}
Following standard federated benchmarking practices~\cite{hsu2019measuring,wang2022fedod}, nominal training traffic is partitioned across $M = 3$ edge gateways using a Dirichlet distribution $\text{Dir}(\alpha)$ parameterized by concentration parameter $\alpha$. Lower values of $\alpha$ enforce severe non-identical distribution skew ($\alpha = 0.1$ represents extreme multi-tenant disjointness, while $\alpha = 5.0$ approaches uniform IID distribution). In standard evaluations, $\alpha$ is set to $0.5$. Testing streams contain $5\%$ anomalous contamination.

\subsubsection{Baseline Hierarchy}
We benchmark Fed-LUNAR against a 3-tier hierarchy of competitive baselines:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Tier 1 (Canonical Federated Graph)}: \emph{Naive Fed-LUNAR} (Standard FedAvg on LUNAR with fixed $\epsilon = 0.1$).
    \item \textbf{Tier 2 (SOTA Representation \& Gradient Surgery)}: \emph{FedAutoEncoder} (4-layer bottleneck reconstruction NIDS~\cite{sakurada2014anomaly}), \emph{FedProx-LUNAR} ($\mu = 0.01$ proximal regularization~\cite{li2020federated}), and \emph{PCGrad-FedLUNAR} (Pairwise projecting conflicting gradients~\cite{yu2020gradient}).
    \item \textbf{Tier 3 (Analytical Bound)}: \emph{LOC-NFST Bound} (Local Orthogonal Component Null Foley-Sammon Transform~\cite{nguyen2024locnfst}, representing the theoretical closed-form upper bound).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Master Benchmark Results and Analysis}
\label{subsec:master_benchmark}

Table~\ref{tab:master_benchmark} summarizes the primary empirical results across all four benchmark datasets. Several critical scientific findings emerge:

\subsubsection{Superiority Over Naive Distance Ranking}
On datasets characterized by complex multi-point attack distributions (\texttt{BoTIoT}, \texttt{CICIoT2023}, and \texttt{N\_BaIoT}), Fed-LUNAR decisively outperforms Naive Fed-LUNAR. On \texttt{BoTIoT}, Fed-LUNAR achieves an F1-Score of \textbf{93.14\%}, compared to only \textbf{71.21\%} for Naive Fed-LUNAR---an absolute improvement of \textbf{+21.93\%}. On high-throughput \texttt{CICIoT2023}, Fed-LUNAR achieves \textbf{82.48\%} F1 vs. \textbf{65.44\%} for Naive Fed-LUNAR (\textbf{+17.04\%}). On 115-dimensional \texttt{N\_BaIoT}, Fed-LUNAR delivers \textbf{97.54\%} F1 vs. \textbf{81.45\%} for Naive Fed-LUNAR (\textbf{+16.09\%}). These substantial gains directly validate our theoretical formulation: eliminating cross-manifold intrusion via CMNP and aligning conflicting gradients via DROGA prevents model stagnation.

\subsubsection{Defensible Victory Over FedAutoEncoder and LOC-NFST}
While FedAutoEncoder performs well on basic volumetric floods (\texttt{BoTIoT}), on high-dimensional multi-stage attacks (\texttt{N\_BaIoT}), Fed-LUNAR surpasses FedAutoEncoder by \textbf{+6.83\%} in F1-Score ($97.54\%$ vs. $90.71\%$) and on \texttt{CICIoT2023} by \textbf{+6.94\%} ($82.48\%$ vs. $75.54\%$). More importantly, LOC-NFST suffers catastrophic memory overhead, consuming $434.8$\,MB on \texttt{BoTIoT}, $839.1$\,MB on \texttt{CICIoT2023}, and $958.2$\,MB on \texttt{N\_BaIoT}. In stark contrast, Fed-LUNAR operates within an ultra-lean memory envelope of \textbf{49.6\,MB to 67.1\,MB RAM}---a staggering \textbf{88.6\% to 93.0\% memory reduction} that makes deployment on commodity edge routers feasible.

\subsection{Dirichlet Non-IID Sensitivity Analysis}
\label{subsec:sensitivity}

To systematically investigate robustness against varying degrees of statistical client heterogeneity, we execute a parameter sweep over Dirichlet concentration $\alpha \in \{0.1, 0.5, 1.0, 5.0\}$ spanning 32 independent federated training runs on \texttt{BoTIoT} and \texttt{CICIoT2023}. The results are compiled in Table~\ref{tab:dirichlet_sweep}.

\subsubsection{Resilience at Extreme Heterogeneity ($\alpha = 0.1$)}
Under extreme statistical skew ($\alpha = 0.1$), client manifolds are nearly completely disconnected. On \texttt{CICIoT2023}, Naive Fed-LUNAR collapses to an F1-Score of \textbf{60.00\%} and Detection Rate of \textbf{69.70\%}. In contrast, Fed-LUNAR maintains an F1-Score of \textbf{70.65\%} and Detection Rate of \textbf{83.33\%}---a notable \textbf{+10.65\% F1 margin}. As $\alpha$ increases to $5.0$ (moderate IID), Fed-LUNAR scales smoothly to \textbf{94.79\%} F1 on \texttt{BoTIoT}, while Naive Fed-LUNAR plateaus at $73.11\%$.

\subsubsection{Dynamics of Gradient Conflict Mitigation}
The empirical conflict ratio logs in Table~\ref{tab:dirichlet_sweep} reveal that opposing gradient angles ($\inner{g_i}{g_j} < 0$) occur in \textbf{30.0\% to 70.0\%} of federated rounds. Naive FedAvg ignores these conflicts, resulting in the annihilation documented in Theorem~\ref{thm:gradient_cancellation}. DROGA actively detects these conflicts and resolves them via dual quadratic programming, sustaining continuous loss descent across all rounds.

\subsection{Component Ablation Studies}
\label{subsec:ablation}

To rigorously quantify the standalone efficacy of Cross-Manifold Negative Purging (CMNP) and Distance-Ranking Orthogonal Gradient Alignment (DROGA), we evaluate two controlled ablation variants across all four datasets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{Ablation\_FedLUNAR\_NoCMNP}: Preserves MSSP and DROGA, but disables sketch-based purging, permitting unconstrained synthetic negative generation.
    \item \texttt{Ablation\_FedLUNAR\_NoDROGA}: Preserves MSSP and CMNP, but replaces server quadratic programming with standard FedAvg gradient averaging.
\end{enumerate}

The empirical ablation results are detailed in Table~\ref{tab:ablation}.

\subsubsection{Crucial Impact of CMNP Filtering}
The removal of CMNP triggers a catastrophic collapse in detection performance across three of the four datasets:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On \texttt{BoTIoT}, disabling CMNP precipitates a \textbf{-26.23\% collapse} in F1-Score (plunging from $93.14\%$ to $66.91\%$).
    \item On \texttt{CICIoT2023}, F1-Score drops by \textbf{-8.82\%} ($82.48\% \to 73.66\%$), and Detection Rate drops from $83.53\%$ to $75.50\%$.
    \item On \texttt{N\_BaIoT}, F1-Score drops by \textbf{-9.79\%} ($97.54\% \to 87.75\%$), and AUC drops from $99.79\%$ to $93.41\%$.
\end{itemize}
These empirical drops prove that cross-manifold negative invasion is an active, destructive phenomenon in Non-IID environments. Without FSDS and CMNP, synthetic negatives poison peer clients' legitimate decision manifolds, severely degrading classification boundaries.

\subsubsection{Role of DROGA Optimization}
Disabling DROGA while retaining CMNP produces a measurable performance degradation on \texttt{CICIoT2023} (\textbf{-2.01\%} F1) and \texttt{N\_BaIoT} (\textbf{-0.39\%} F1). While CMNP cleans the synthetic dataset, DROGA provides the final optimization safeguard, ensuring that heterogeneous client updates do not destructively interfere at the parameter server.

\subsection{Edge Systems Feasibility and Hardware Profiling}
\label{subsec:hardware_profiling}

In high-throughput edge environments, an intrusion detection system must satisfy rigorous line-rate throughput and memory constraints. Table~\ref{tab:master_benchmark} includes real hardware execution profiling on our testbed:

\subsubsection{Line-Rate Streaming Latency}
Fed-LUNAR achieves an ultra-low per-packet inference latency ranging from \textbf{0.0008\,ms (0.8\,$\mu$s) on \texttt{BoTIoT}} to \textbf{0.0020\,ms (2.0\,$\mu$s) on 115-feature \texttt{N\_BaIoT}}. This corresponds to a sustained throughput exceeding \textbf{500,000 to 1,250,000 packets per second}, enabling deterministic wire-speed traffic inspection on 1\,Gbps and 10\,Gbps industrial edge links without packet queue overflow.

\subsubsection{Edge Memory Working Set}
While analytical spectral methods (LOC-NFST) require $434.8$\,MB to $958.2$\,MB of system RAM to maintain kernel covariance and projection structures, Fed-LUNAR requires only \textbf{49.6\,MB to 67.1\,MB RAM} across all benchmarks. This represents an active memory reduction of \textbf{88.6\% to 93.0\%}, proving that Fed-LUNAR is fully viable for deployment on memory-constrained IoT edge gateways (e.g., Raspberry Pi 4 with 1\,GB RAM).
